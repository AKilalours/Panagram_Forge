# Image Data Specification v1 (FORGE-IMAGE)

Status: **draft, not frozen.** Freeze before writing ingestion code, the way
`data_spec_v1.md` was frozen before the text pipeline.

Scope: the smallest image track that tests the same research question as the text track.
Not a second product. If a decision here does not serve the question "does matched
synthetic data generalize better than unmatched", it is out of scope for v1.

---

## 1. The question, restated for images

Does generating AI images **matched** to real photographs (same scene, same composition,
same lighting) train a detector that generalizes better to unseen image generators than
training on unmatched AI images, while keeping the false-positive rate on real photographs
extremely low?

Identical in structure to the text question. That is deliberate: the two tracks share the
Failure Atlas, the release gate, the registry and the evaluation regimes, and they are
only worth building together if they are answering the same question.

---

## 2. THE CONFOUND THAT WOULD RUIN THIS

Read this before anything else.

Real photographs arrive as JPEG, from a camera, resized by a hosting service, often
recompressed several times. Diffusion output arrives as a lossless PNG at exactly
1024x1024, never compressed, never resized, with no camera pipeline behind it.

A detector trained on those two populations will reach near-perfect accuracy **without
ever looking at whether the image is generated**. It will have learned JPEG quantisation
artifacts, resampling signatures and aspect-ratio priors. It will then collapse the moment
someone screenshots an AI image or uploads a real photo as PNG.

This is the image-track equivalent of a text detector learning the topic, and it is worse,
because it is invisible to accuracy, AUROC and every headline metric.

**Mitigation, mandatory in v1:**

1. Every image on both sides passes through one identical normalisation before it is ever
   seen by the model: decode, resize the short side to a fixed length, centre crop to a
   square, re-encode as JPEG at a single fixed quality, strip all metadata.
2. The normalisation is applied by one function, used by ingestion, generation, training
   and serving. Not reimplemented per call site.
3. A **negative control** is required before any result is reported: train the same model
   on human images only, split into two arbitrary halves labelled 0 and 1. If it does
   better than chance, the pipeline is leaking a source signature and every other number
   is void.
4. Aspect ratio and resolution are recorded but never fed to the model in v1.

The negative control is the single most important test in the image track. It is cheap and
it is the only thing that distinguishes a real result from a pipeline artifact.

## 3. Human image source

**Open Images V7.** Verified 2026-09-02 from the official facts and figures page:

- Approximately 9.16 million images: 8,992,648 train, 41,620 validation, 125,436 test.
- Images are listed as **CC BY 2.0**, with an explicit disclaimer from the maintainers:
  they tried to identify CC BY images but make no representations or warranties about the
  license status of any individual image, and the user must verify each one.
- Annotations are **CC BY 4.0**, by Google LLC.

Consequences for FORGE, which are not optional:

- The disclaimer means per-image license status is **unverified upstream**. FORGE records
  the license string and attribution for every image it ingests and treats them as claims
  carried from the source, not as facts it has established.
- FORGE **never redistributes image pixels.** The publishable artifact is a manifest of
  image ids, source URLs, recorded license, attribution, perceptual hash and metadata.
  Same rule as the text track, same reason.
- v1 target: **20,000 human images**, sampled deterministically by a salted hash of the
  image id, so the sample is reproducible and independent of download order.

Rejected for v1, with reasons recorded so the decision is not revisited by accident:

- Scraping the web: no provenance, and unacceptable for a project shown to a company.
- LAION: known content issues and a licensing position that would need its own review.
- Personal photos: not redistributable, not reproducible by a reader.

## 4. AI image sources

Two populations, both required, for the reason the text track learned the hard way.

**A. Synthetic mirrors.** Human image, described by a vision-language model, regenerated
from that description. This is the arm under test.

**B. Real-world AI images.** Images generated by other people with other pipelines. A
detector trained only on our own generation pipeline learns **our** artifacts. Population B
is what tells us whether it learned anything transferable.

Held-in generators (used for training data):

| family | model | license | note |
|---|---|---|---|
| sdxl | stabilityai/stable-diffusion-xl-base-1.0 | CreativeML Open RAIL++-M | fits 24GB comfortably |
| sd15 | runwayml/stable-diffusion-v1-5 | CreativeML Open RAIL-M | small, fast, different era |
| flux-schnell | black-forest-labs/FLUX.1-schnell | Apache-2.0, gated | 12B, tight on 24GB, may need quantisation |

Held out (never used for training data, only evaluation):

| family | purpose |
|---|---|
| a fourth open model, chosen at eval time | unseen-generator regime |

Every generated image records: family, model id, pinned revision, scheduler, steps,
guidance scale, seed, resolution, prompt version, source human id. Same discipline as
`generators_minimal.yaml`, and `pin-revisions` must be extended to cover it.

**FLUX.1-schnell is gated**, verified 2026-09-02: access requires agreeing to share
contact information on HuggingFace. Its Apache-2.0 license permits commercial use, and its
out-of-scope-use section prohibits non-consensual, illegal and disinformation content.
FORGE generates from Open Images captions only, which does not approach those categories,
but the constraint is recorded rather than assumed away.

## 5. The mirror engine

```
human image -> VLM -> structured caption -> image generator -> AI mirror
```

The VLM caption is the ONLY thing that crosses from the human image to the generated one.
No pixels, no latents, no img2img initialisation. Under img2img the "mirror" would contain
the original image's structure and the detector would be learning a denoising signature,
not a generation signature.

Caption fields, fixed: scene, primary objects, spatial relations, composition, lighting,
colour palette, camera perspective, apparent time of day.

Candidate captioner: a small open VLM that fits alongside a diffusion model on one 24GB
card. Model choice is deferred to a measured comparison on 50 images, not a guess.

**Mirror validity checks**, the image analogue of the text length-ratio check:

- CLIP similarity between the caption and the generated image above a floor: the generator
  actually rendered what was asked.
- CLIP similarity between the generated image and the SOURCE image within a band. Too low
  means the mirror is unrelated; too high means the generator reproduced a memorised image
  and the pair is contaminated rather than matched.
- Rejection reasons recorded per image, exactly as the text validator does.

Expect a rejection rate. The text mirror arm rejected about 40% on first pass and retries
recovered only a fifth of those, so **assume rejections are systematic, budget for them,
and do not rely on retries.**

## 6. Composites, for the localisation head

Take a human image, inpaint one region with a generator, record the mask. Ground truth is
then known per pixel and the localisation head can be trained and measured with IoU, Dice
and boundary error rather than eyeballed.

Composites are **not** in the v1 minimum. They are the first thing to add after the global
head produces an honest number.

---

## 7. Splits

Reuse `forge.common.splits` unchanged. The grouping key is the source human image id, so a
photograph, its mirrors and any composite built from it all land in the same split. The
text track's leakage rule applies without modification, which is the payoff for having
written it modality-agnostically.

---

## 8. Adversarial operations

JPEG at several qualities, resize, crop, screenshot simulation, blur, sharpen, metadata
strip, and later inpainting and outpainting. Each is applied to a held-out AI set and the
change in false-negative rate is reported per operation.

Note the interaction with section 2: since normalisation already re-encodes everything as
JPEG at a fixed quality, the adversarial suite must be applied BEFORE normalisation, in the
same order a real upload would experience.

---

## 9. Metrics

Global head: FPR at a fixed budget as the primary metric, then FNR, AUROC, ECE.
Localisation head: IoU, Dice, pixel precision and recall, boundary error.
Robustness: delta FNR per adversarial operation.
Plus the negative control from section 2, reported as a pass/fail gate, not a number.

## 10. Compute estimate, single RTX 4090

Rough, to be replaced by a measurement the way the text estimate was:

| stage | scale | estimate |
|---|---|---|
| download and normalise humans | 20,000 images | 1-2 h, network bound |
| VLM captioning | 20,000 images | 2-4 h |
| mirror generation, SDXL | 20,000 images | 6-10 h |
| unmatched control generation | 20,000 images | 6-10 h |
| DINOv3 training, two arms | 40,000 images each | 2-3 h |

So roughly **20 to 30 GPU-hours**, against about 3 for the entire text experiment. That
ratio is the honest reason the text track came first.

---

## 11. What v1 does NOT include

Video, audio, face-swap detection, C2PA verification, a browser extension, and any claim
about matching a commercial image detector's accuracy. Each of those makes the project
wider and none of them make the result more credible.
